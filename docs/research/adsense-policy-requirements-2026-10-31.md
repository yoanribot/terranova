# Google AdSense policy requirements

Verified: 2026-10-31. Sources: official Google policy and product-help pages. This note defines assessment criteria; the companion live-site audit must supply observations. It does not certify compliance or identify Google's private review signals.

## Mandatory rules relevant to a dental-clinic website

- **Program conduct and navigation:** publishers must follow both AdSense Program Policies and Google Publisher Policies. Artificial impressions, own-ad clicks, paid-to-click traffic, encouragement to click, disguised ads, deceptive navigation and ad-only/non-content pages are prohibited. Navigation must be usable. Traffic acquisition and invalid activity cannot be fully assessed from public pages. [AdSense Program policies](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/48182?hl=en)
- **Inventory value:** Google ads cannot monetize screens without publisher content, with low-value content, under construction, or primarily serving behavioral/navigation purposes. Error/thank-you/dead-end screens are examples. Automatically generated material without manual review or curation is also problematic. The presence of an ad loader alone is not proof an ad was served on a particular screen. [Screens without publisher-content](https://support.google.com/publisherpolicies/answer/11112688?hl=en)
- **Original value and rights:** copied, embedded or rewritten third-party material needs substantive added value; slight paraphrases alone do not establish it. Copyright obligations remain separate. A stock image, generic wording or AI-assisted draft alone does not prove copying or a violation. [Replicated content](https://support.google.com/publisherpolicies/answer/11190248?hl=en)
- **Ad density:** ads and other paid promotional material must not exceed publisher content. Whitespace, headers, footers and links to other content do not count as publisher content for this comparison. A clinic's promotion of its own services is not automatically equivalent to third-party paid advertising. [Ad-to-content balance](https://support.google.com/publisherpolicies/answer/11169917?hl=en)
- **User interactions:** ads cannot sit adjacent to or overlap navigation/actions in ways that cause unintended clicks, seriously interfere with consuming content, or require an ad click to leave. Check mobile, sticky buttons and forms when actual ads render. [Ads interfering](https://support.google.com/publisherpolicies/answer/11035030?hl=en)
- **Truthful publisher and content information:** Google prohibits misleading representation of publisher/content creator, false endorsements and deceptive claims. General prohibited categories include illegal content, copyright abuse, dangerous/derogatory content and sexual exploitation. The policy framework also incorporates Search spam requirements for ad-bearing screens. [Google Publisher Policies](https://support.google.com/publisherpolicies/answer/10502938?hl=en)
- **Health claims:** harmful health claims, or major-health-crisis material contradicting authoritative scientific consensus, are prohibited. Dental content is not categorically prohibited. Unsupported promises should be checked by a qualified clinician; lack of a byline alone is not the named harmful-health-claims violation. [Unreliable and harmful claims](https://support.google.com/publisherpolicies/answer/11188580?hl=en)
- **Health-based personalization:** Google code/platforms must not select/target personalized ads or build/use audiences from actual or inferred health or medical information. Consent does not override this sensitive-information prohibition. Advertising on a health page is not itself proof of prohibited audience targeting. [Personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/publisherpolicies/answer/15101728?hl=en)
- **User identification:** do not send Google data it could recognize as personally identifiable information. Do not put patient names, email, telephone numbers or contact-form/medical details in Google ad/analytics payloads or tracked URLs. [Identifying users](https://support.google.com/publisherpolicies/answer/10436913?hl=en-GB)
- **Privacy disclosures:** a privacy policy must explain actual Google-related collection, sharing and use, relevant technologies/identifiers, and third parties placing/reading cookies or using web beacons/IP addresses for ads. A generic policy link alone does not prove accurate disclosure. Google's data-use link is one available disclosure aid. [Privacy disclosures within Publisher Policies](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/10502938)

## European consent: required distinctions

For EEA, UK and Swiss users, the EU User Consent Policy requires legally valid consent for cookies/local storage where legally required and for collecting/sharing/using data for personalized advertising. Publishers seeking consent must retain records, explain withdrawal, identify relevant parties and provide accessible information about their data use. [EU User Consent Policy](https://www.google.com/about/company/user-consent-policy/)

A Google-certified CMP integrated with the IAB TCF is required for **personalized ads** in these regions. The current requirements page expressly says traffic from a non-certified CMP may remain eligible for non-personalized or limited ads where supported. Do not describe certification as an unconditional requirement for every possible limited-ad configuration. CMP certification does not establish legal compliance; a loading Funding Choices script does not establish that the published message, accept/reject actions, withdrawal, or tag behavior work. [Google CMP requirements](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/13554116?hl=en)

Google's European message revocation guidance requires a footer withdrawal link and says Google automatically adds it to **approved** sites containing AdSense code. Its absence on an unapproved site alone therefore does not prove that automatic injection is broken; consent and withdrawal still need validation for the actual configuration. [Consent revocation](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/10959060?hl=en-GB)

## Implementation and approval guidance

Google's connection instructions accept three verification methods: the AdSense script between the head tags; a valid root ads.txt record; or a publisher-account meta tag in the head. Therefore a body-injected script differs from the documented placement but does not alone prove ownership verification must fail where valid alternatives exist. Code correctness and approval of the entire site are separate. [Connect your site to AdSense](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/7584263?hl=en-GB)

AdSense readiness guidance recommends distinctive original material, meaningful visitor value, and clear working navigation. Specialist knowledge and useful additions matter; comments are optional suggestions, not prerequisites. The page specifies no mandatory numeric article/word/traffic minimum. [Pages ready for AdSense](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/7299563?hl=en)

Google may review every page. Its disapproval help distinguishes insufficient content, content quality, prohibited content, navigation, traffic sources and unsupported language. Address the actual public deployment before requesting another review. A clinic brochure may have real value but still fail Google's original-rich-content assessment; describe that as an evidence-based risk, not a confirmed private Google decision. [Account not approved](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/81904?linkId=63907539)

## Limits of a public audit

Public review can establish returned content, routes, scripts, disclosures, visible consent controls, observed storage/requests and rendered ad placement. It cannot establish Google account approval, genuine consent records across all regions, all traffic sources, click history, ad personalization settings, ownership of images, or professional credentials without further evidence. Report confirmed defects separately from risks and unverified account settings.
